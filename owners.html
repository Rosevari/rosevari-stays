  1. We call the agent.

    We ask whether the owner is open to a short-stay lease at the advertised rent. If not, no problem.

  2. The owner agrees in writing.

    Consent goes into the lease as a one-page addendum their solicitor can change.

  3. We set the home up.

    Furnished, insured, a smart lock fitted and a local cleaner booked before the first guest.

  4. Verified guests stay.

    Professionals and visitors, for a few nights up to several months.

Owner questions.

The ones we hear most, answered straight.

Will guests throw parties or damage the place?

Totally fair concern. Every guest must verify their government ID, and we check their Airbnb profile, reviews and history before we accept a booking. No parties or events, and quiet hours from 10pm to 7am.

If anything is damaged, we fix it at our cost. Airbnb bookings carry Airbnb's host damage protection, and our own short-stay insurance is in place before the first guest. We answer guest messages ourselves, day and night, so issues are handled straight away.

The owner would rather have a long-term tenant.

Totally fair. Most owners would. The thing is, we are a long-term tenant. We sign a standard lease for 12, 18 or 24 months, pay the full rent on the 1st of every month, and treat the home like a business asset, not a crash pad.

The owner gets the same security as with a regular tenant, plus a professional clean after every stay. And because this is a business for us, if the home works well we are very likely to renew. That means less turnover and fewer vacant weeks.

Why wouldn't the owner just do this themselves?

They could. But running short stays is a job: guest messages, a clean after every checkout, pricing, reviews, maintenance and the occasional 2am lockout. Most owners want the rent without a second job. We take on all of it, and the risk of empty nights.

What if you don't get bookings?

The owner is still paid in full on the 1st. Empty nights are our cost. We also only apply for homes where similar short stays nearby earn at least twice the rent, so the numbers work before we sign.

Is this allowed?

Yes, with the owner's written consent. We sign the standard lease with a one-page consent addendum covering the guest limit, house rules, insurance, cleaning, repairs, inspections and rent. The owner's solicitor or agent can review and change it.

The rent is always the advertised rent, never more, as Victorian and Queensland tenancy law requires. If the building has owners corporation or body corporate rules about short stays, we check them before we apply.

How is the home insured?

Standard landlord and renter policies do not cover short stays. So we take out a specialist short-stay policy through an insurance broker, with $20 million public liability, noting the owner's interest. It is arranged before the first guest. The owner gets the certificate of currency within 7 days of signing, and again at each renewal.

How long have you been doing this?

We are setting up our first homes now. We would rather say that plainly than oversell it. That is why every promise on this site goes into the signed addendum, not just this website. You deal with Jordan directly.
